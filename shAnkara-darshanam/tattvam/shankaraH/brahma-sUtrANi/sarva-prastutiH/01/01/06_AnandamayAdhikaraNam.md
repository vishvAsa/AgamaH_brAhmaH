## सङ्गतिः

संसारी ब्रह्म वाऽऽनन्दमयः, संसार्ययं भवेत् ॥  
विकारार्थमयट्शब्दात्प्रियाद्यवयवोक्तितः ॥ १५ ॥  
अभ्यासोपक्रमादिभ्यो ब्रह्मानन्दमयो भवेत् ॥  
प्राचुर्यार्थो मयट्शब्दः प्रियाद्याः स्युरुपाधिगाः ॥ १६ ॥  
--वैयासिकन्यायमाला

<details><summary>ज्ञानानन्द-भारती - द्राविडी</summary>

आऩन्दमयऩ् ऎऩ्बदु संसारिया (जीवऩा)? अल्लदु पिरह्ममा? माऱुदलैच् चॊल्लुम् “मयट्” ऎऩ्ऱ सप्तमिरुप् पदिऩालुम्, पिरियम् मुदलाऩ अवयवङ्गळै सॊल्लियिरुप् पदिऩालुम् इदु संसारिदाऩ् आगुम्।
</details>

<details><summary>ज्ञानानन्द-भारती - द्राविडी</summary>

अडिक्कडि सॊल्लुदल्, तुवक्कम् मुदलाऩ वैगळि लिरुन्दु (कवऩिक्कुम् पोदु) आऩन्दमयऩ् पिरह्ममागत् ताऩ् इरुक्क वेण्डुम्। "मयट्" ऎऩ्ऱ सप्तम् अदिगम् ऎऩ्ऱ अर्त्तत्तिल् सॊल्लप्पट्टिरुक्किऱदु। पिरियम् मुदलियवै उबादिगळैच् चेर्न्दवैयागुम्।
</details>

अन्याङ्गं स्वप्रधानं वा ब्रह्म पुच्छमिति श्रुतम् ॥  
स्यादानन्दमयस्याङ्गं पुच्छेऽङ्गत्वप्रसिद्धितः ॥ १७ ॥  
लाङ्गूलासम्भवादत्र पुच्छेनाऽऽधारलक्षणा ॥  
आनन्दमयजीवोऽस्मिन्नाश्रितोऽतः प्रधानता ॥ १८ ॥  
--वैयासिकन्यायमाला

### सगुणम् ब्रह्म
<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः</summary>

‘जन्माद्य् अस्य यतः’ इत्य् आरभ्य  
‘श्रुतत्वाच् च’ इत्य्-एवम्-अन्तैः सूत्रैर्  
यान्य् उदाहृतानि वेदान्त-वाक्यानि,  
तेषां सर्वज्ञः सर्व-शक्तिर् ईश्वरो  
जगतो जन्म-स्थिति-लय-कारणम् इत्य् एतस्यार्थस्य प्रतिपादकत्वं न्याय-पूर्वकं प्रतिपादितम्।
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

In what precedes we have shown, availing ourselves of appropriate arguments, that the Vedānta-texts exhibited under Sūtras I, I-II, are capable of proving that the all-knowing, all-powerful Lord is the cause of the origin, subsistence, and dissolution of the world.
</details>

<details><summary>मूलम्</summary>

‘जन्माद्यस्य यतः’ इत्यारभ्य ‘श्रुतत्वाच्च’ इत्येवमन्तैः सूत्रैर्यान्युदाहृतानि वेदान्तवाक्यानि, तेषां सर्वज्ञः सर्वशक्तिरीश्वरो जगतो जन्मस्थितिलयकारणमित्येतस्यार्थस्य प्रतिपादकत्वं न्यायपूर्वकं प्रतिपादितम्।
</details>

<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः</summary>

> गति-सामान्योपन्यासेन च  
सर्वे वेदान्ताश् चेतन-कारण-वादिन 

इति व्याख्यातम् ।
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

And we have explained, by pointing to the prevailing uniformity of view (I, 10), that all Vedānta-texts whatever maintain an intelligent cause.
</details>

<details><summary>मूलम्</summary>

</details>

### द्विरूपम् ब्रह्म
<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः</summary>

> अतः परस्य ग्रन्थस्य किम् उत्थानम्? 

इति, उच्यते —  
द्वि-रूपं हि ब्रह्मावगम्यते —  
नाम-रूप-विकार-भेदोपाधि-विशिष्टम् ,  
तद्-विपरीतं च सर्वोपाधि-विवर्जितम् ।+++(5)+++
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

The question might therefore be asked, 'What reason is there for the subsequent part of the Vedānta-sūtras?' (as the chief point is settled already.) To this question we reply as follows: Brahman is apprehended under two forms; in the first place as qualified by limiting conditions owing to the multiformity of the evolutions of name and form (i.e. the multiformity of the created world; in the second place as being the opposite of this, i.e. free from all limiting conditions whatever. Compare
</details>

<details><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अतः परस्य ग्रन्थस्य किमुत्थानमिति, उच्यते — द्विरूपं हि ब्रह्मावगम्यते — नामरूपविकारभेदोपाधिविशिष्टम् , तद्विपरीतं च सर्वोपाधिविवर्जितम् ।
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

the following passages: Br̥. Up. IV, 5, 15, 'For where there is duality as it were, then one sees the other; but when the Self only is all this, how should he see another?' Cḥ. Up. VII, 24, 1, 'Where one sees nothing else, hears nothing else, understands nothing else, that is the greatest. Where one sees something else, hears something else, understands something else, that is the little. The greatest is immortal; the little is mortal;' Taitt. Ār. III, 12, 7, 'The wise one, who having produced all forms and made all names, sits calling (the things by their names [^fn_104]);'

<details><summary>Translation - Notes</summary>

[^fn_104]: 62:1 The wise one, i.e. the highest Self; which as jīvātman is conversant with the names and forms of individual things.

</details>

अमृतस्य परं सेतुं

सम्पूर्णम् अन्यत्’ 

इति चैवं सहस्रशो विद्याऽविद्या-विषय-भेदेन  
ब्रह्मणो द्विरूपतां दर्शयन्ति वाक्यानि ।
</details>

<details><summary>विश्वास-टिप्पनी</summary>

द्विरूपतोहां विना मुख्यार्थेनैव निर्वाहे सम्भवे सति,  
नोचितोऽयं द्विरूपतोहः। 
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

Śv. Up. VI, 19, 'Who is without parts, without actions, tranquil, without faults, without taint,the highest bridge of immortality, like a fire that has consumed its fuel;' Br̥. Up. II, 3, 6, 'Not so, not so;' Br̥. Up. III, 8, 8, 'It is neither coarse nor fine, neither short nor long;' and 'defective is one place, perfect the other.' All these passages, with many others, declare Brahman to possess a double nature, according as it is the object either of Knowledge or of Nescience.
</details>

#### अविद्यावस्था

</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

As long as it is the object of Nescience, there are applied to it the categories of devotee, object of devotion, and the like [^fn_105].
</details>

[^fn_105]: 62:2 I.e. it is looked upon as the object of the devotion of the individual souls; while in reality all those souls and Brahman are one.
</details>

##### आत्मोपाधि-भेदः
<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः</summary>

> ‘तस्य य आत्मानम् आविस्तरां वेद’ (ऐ. आ. २। ३। २। १) 

इत्यत्र।
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

Although one and the same Self is hidden in all beings movable as well as immovable, yet owing to the gradual rise of excellence of the minds which form the limiting conditions (of the Self), Scripture declares that the Self, although eternally unchanging and uniform, reveals itself [^fn_107] in a graduated series of beings, and so appears in forms of various dignity and power; compare, for instance (Ait. Ār. II, 3, 2, 1), 'He who knows the higher manifestation of the Self in him [^fn_108],' &c.
</details>

<details><summary>Translation - Notes</summary>

[^fn_107]: 63:1 Ānanda Giri reads āvishṭasya for āvishkr̥tasya.
[^fn_108]: 63:2 Cp. the entire passage. All things are manifestations of the highest Self under certain limiting conditions, but occupying different places in an ascending scale. ### उपास्यत्व-ज्ञेयत्वे
<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः</summary>

उपाधि-विशेष-द्वारेणोपदिश्यमानम् अप्य्

</details>

### चेतनान्तर-व्यावर्तनम्

## आनन्दमयो ऽमुख्यात्मा?

### न मुख्यात्मा
#### शारिरत्वम्
<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः</summary>

अथापि स्यात् 

> सर्वान्तरत्वाद् आनन्दमयो मुख्य एवात्मेति;  

न स्यात् - प्रियाद्य्-अवयव-योगाच् ,  
छारीरत्व-श्रवणाच् च।
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

To the objection that even thus the Self consisting of bliss may be considered as the primary Self, since it is stated to be the innermost of all, he replies that this cannot be admitted, because the Self of bliss is declared to have joy and so on for its limbs, and because it is said to be embodied.
</details>

<details><summary>मूलम्</summary>

अथापि स्यात्सर्वान्तरत्वादानन्दमयो मुख्य एवात्मेति; न स्यात्प्रियाद्यवयवयोगाच्छारीरत्वश्रवणाच्च।
</details>

<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः</summary>

मुख्यश् चेद् आत्मा स्यान्,  
न प्रियादि-संस्पर्शः स्यात्।  
इह तु ‘तस्य प्रियम् एव शिरः’ (तै. उ. २। ५। १) इत्यादि श्रूयते।  
शारीरत्वं च श्रूयते — 

> ‘तस्यैष एव शारीर आत्मा  
यः पूर्वस्य’ 

इति।  

> तस्य पूर्वस्य विज्ञानमयस्यैष एव शारीर आत्मा  
य एष आनन्दमय 

इत्यर्थः।
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

If it were identical with the primary Self, joy and the like would not touch it; but the text expressly says 'Joy is its head;' and about its being embodied we read, 'Of that former one this one is the embodied Self' (Taitt. Up. II, 6), i.e. of that former Self of Understanding this Self of bliss is the embodied Self.
</details>

<details><summary>मूलम्</summary>

मुख्यश्चेदात्मा स्यान्न प्रियादिसंस्पर्शः स्यात्। इह तु ‘तस्य प्रियमेव शिरः’ (तै. उ. २। ५। १) इत्यादि श्रूयते। शारीरत्वं च श्रूयते — ‘तस्यैष एव शारीर आत्मा यः पूर्वस्य’ इति। तस्य पूर्वस्य विज्ञानमयस्यैष एव शारीर आत्मा य एष आनन्दमय इत्यर्थः।
</details>

‘रसो वै सः’ (तै. उ. २। ७। १)  
इति तस्यैव रसत्वम् उक्त्वा,  
उच्यते — 

> ‘रसँ ह्य् एवायं लब्ध्वा ऽऽनन्दी भवति।  
को ह्य् एवान्यात्, कः प्राण्यात्।  
यद् एष आकाश आनन्दो न स्यात्।  
एष ह्य् एवानन्दयाति’ (तै. उ. २। ७। १) 

> ‘सैषानन्दस्य मीमाँसा भवति’ (तै. उ. २। ८। १) 

> ‘एतम् आनन्दमयम् आत्मानम् उपसङ्क्रामति’ (तै. उ. २। ८। ५)

न बिभेति कुतश्चन’ (तै. उ. २। ९। १)

इति च।
</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

So Taitt. Up. II, 7, where, after the clause 'That is flavour'--which refers back to the Self consisting of bliss, and declares it to be of the nature of flavour--we read, 'For only after having perceived flavour can any one perceive delight. Who could breathe, who could breathe forth if that Bliss existed not in the ether (of the heart)? For he alone causes blessedness;' and again, II, 8, 'Now this is an examination of Bliss;' 'He reaches that Self consisting of Bliss;' and again, II, 9, 'He who knows the Bliss of Brahman fears nothing;' and in addition, 'He understood that Bliss is Brahman' (III, 6).

<details><summary>मूलम्</summary>

</details>

<details open><summary>विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः</summary>

</details>

<details><summary>Translation</summary>

As this śloka, without any reference to the Self of bliss, states the advantage and disadvantage connected with the knowledge of the being and non-being of Brahman only, we conclude that the clause, 'Brahman is the tail, the support,' represents Brahman as the chief matter (not as a merely subordinate matter).
</details>
